01 / The humanitarian question

What remains after conflict can remain dangerous for years.

Unexploded ordnance can endanger residents, recovery workers and rebuilding efforts. Ground-based detection puts people close to suspected hazards, while isolated sensors can struggle with hidden objects, heat variation and soil clutter.

Surface uncertainty

Thermal appearance can vary with weather, soil temperature and occlusion.

Subsurface uncertainty

Ground-penetrating radar can detect anomalies, but clutter may resemble buried threats.

Human exposure

Remote observation could help prioritize professional assessment, without replacing clearance teams.

02 / Research methodology

Two trained detectors. One informed decision.

The project trains separate detection models on thermal images and GPR radargrams. A hybrid fusion stage combines their outputs to seek more reliable hazard identification.

01 / DATA

Acquire & prepare

2,095 thermal images and 526 GPR samples; annotated data, normalization, resizing and augmentation.

02 / MODELS

Train separately

YOLOv11 in the final thesis, with 70% train, 15% validation and 15% test splits; compare independent detection quality.

03 / HYBRID FUSION

Reconcile evidence

Weighted Boxes Fusion with decision-level weighting combines matching confidence from both sensing channels.

04 / EVALUATION

Test the outputs

Assess precision, recall, F1, mAP@50, AUC and localization. Validate against independent unseen conditions.

Why fusion rather than one model?

Thermal imaging responds to surface patterns but cannot reliably see through soil. GPR provides subsurface information but can be affected by soil and background clutter. The study tests whether supporting signals can strengthen decisions when one sensor is ambiguous.

Final score = α × Thermal score + β × Radar support
Study weighting: α = 0.8 · β = 0.2

A model confidence score is not a verified probability that an object is hazardous. The fusion results come from the thesis experiments.

05 / Practical challenges & next steps

A strong research result is the beginning—not the finish line.

Translating controlled testing into operationally useful humanitarian tools requires careful independent assessment.

Improve generalization

Expand datasets across terrain, weather, burial depth and ordnance types; evaluate blind external test sites and cross-dataset performance.

Calibrate multimodal predictions

Check sensor synchronization, registration, and the true reliability of confidence scores before using them for prioritization.

Validate field feasibility

Measure drone payload, power demand, GPR sensing depth, altitude constraints, and inference latency in representative controlled environments.

Keep expert-led safety protocols

Any suspected hazard requires qualified humanitarian mine-action assessment. The system does not determine whether ground is safe or authorize handling or clearance.

Research integrity

What the results do—and don't—establish.

Supported by the thesis

Two independently trained detector streams, a hybrid-fusion evaluation, the datasets and reported quantitative scores.

Not yet established

Deployed airborne field performance, generalization to live conflict sites, certified clearance reliability, or direct comparison to qualified demining teams under equivalent conditions.

The accompanying presentation references YOLOv12 and some different metrics, while the longer final thesis specifies YOLOv11. This website consistently uses the thesis abstract's version and figures. Contact the research team for the version history.
